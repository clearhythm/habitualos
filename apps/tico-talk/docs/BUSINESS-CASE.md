# Business Case — Why Menu Training Matters

A running collection of outside sources backing up the "why" behind Tico
(see [VISION.md](./VISION.md) for the product argument itself) — evidence
that restaurants underinvest in staff menu/knowledge training and that it
has real, if mostly qualitative, business consequences. Add to this list
as new sources turn up; keep each entry to source + dated claims, not
paraphrase-as-fact.

## References

### [Training Staff on Menu Knowledge](https://activemenus.com/training-staff-on-menu-knowledge/) — ActiveMenus

Industry blog post, not a data study — claims below are qualitative, no
sales figures or error-rate stats cited in the source itself. Useful for
articulating the mechanism, not as a cited number.

- **Confidence → presentation**: a server's depth of menu knowledge
  directly shapes how confidently they present it to a guest.
- **Guest embarrassment**: when a server has to leave the table to go ask
  the kitchen/bar a menu question, the guest is left feeling awkward, and
  the venue reads as less put-together.
- **Revenue mechanism**: guests notice and trust confident, knowledgeable
  service, which the article ties to guests spending more.
- **Operational cost**: repeated kitchen/bar trips to answer questions
  cost staff time across the team, not just the one server, and slow
  service overall.
- **Retention/recommendation**: guests who get confident, authoritative
  answers — especially on dietary questions — are more likely to return
  and to recommend the venue to others.

## Competitors

Adjacent products worth tracking — not exhaustive, add as more turn up.

### [Yelli](https://yelliapp.com/)

Founded 2019 (per Erik, 2026-08-19) — pre-dates the current LLM wave, so
it's almost certainly not built AI-native, or if it's bolted something
on since, it isn't a product built around AI from the ground up the way
Tico is. Worth re-checking their site/marketing periodically for signs
they've pivoted toward it, but as of this writing everything below reads
as static content + quiz/verification, not conversational or adaptive.

Digital replacement for training binders/spreadsheets: menus, SOPs, and
policies delivered to staff with built-in quizzes/sign-offs before
someone's cleared for the floor. Day-by-day plans by role, manager
dashboard, cross-platform (desktop/iOS/Android). Markets itself on
onboarding-time claims: new-hire ramp from 2 weeks to 3 days, 10+
hrs/week of manager time saved, 3,500+ staff trained.

Pricing (per Erik, 2026-08-19, not confirmed from the site itself which
gates it behind "Book a Demo"): **$79/month flat, unlimited users**, plus
a **$199 one-time setup fee** (menus/SOPs/company policies organized and
loaded in).

Erik's read: the flat unlimited-user monthly fee is a strange shape —
under-prices a large multi-location chain relative to a single small
restaurant, and gives Yelli no natural expansion revenue as a customer's
staff count grows. Worth deciding deliberately whether Tico prices
per-location, per-active-user, or some other axis rather than defaulting
to copying this. The one-time setup fee, by contrast, makes sense to
Erik — real cost to structure a restaurant's content, one-time not
recurring, a model Tico's own onboarding (feeding it a restaurant's
actual menu) could reasonably borrow.

**How Tico differs**: Yelli is content delivery + quiz/verification —
static material, testing for compliance/sign-off. Tico's bet is
conversational rehearsal (an AI coach that drills judgment and recall
under something closer to real interaction, moving toward voice) rather
than a quiz you pass once. Worth stealing the "ramp time" framing (2
weeks → 3 days) as a stat shape to eventually have our own version of.

**Additional claims** (per a Google AI summary Erik pulled, 2026-08-19 —
secondhand, not verified against a primary source; treat as marketing
copy/case-study anecdote, not audited data):
- **Turnover**: platform promoted as helping "structured operations"
  achieve up to 23% less staff turnover.
- **Check size**: at least one individual client case study reports
  average guest check rising from $92 to $120, attributed to trained
  staff upselling more effectively.
- **Scope/limitations noted by the summary itself**: Yelli is a small,
  bootstrapped company — no independent third-party financial data or
  corporate success metrics beyond its own marketing. Also flagged as
  entirely dependent on active management input: if operators don't keep
  menus/modules updated in the app, it loses effectiveness.

That last limitation is a real point of contrast worth sitting with:
Yelli's content is exactly as fresh as whoever last updated it by hand.
Tico still depends on someone feeding it accurate menu data too, so this
isn't a difference to overclaim — but the 23%-turnover and $92→$120
check-size numbers are exactly the kind of quantified stat this doc is
otherwise missing (see Open Questions) — worth trying to find an
equivalent, verifiable version of both for Tico once there's real usage
data.

**Market read** (Erik, 2026-08-19): founder impression aside, Yelli has a
"reasonable presence," which by itself is a signal they're doing *some*
real business — but Erik's sense is they haven't done especially well.
Read as: real, direct competitor, worth tracking, not evidence the
category is crowded or saturated.
